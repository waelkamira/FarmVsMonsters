# Farm Vs Monsters — Secondary Campaign Map Plan
## Comparative study of Plants vs. Zombies 2 Adventure Mode

**Document role:** Secondary / alternative campaign plan for comparison and design learning.  
**Primary authority:** `GLOBAL_GAME_SUCCESS_MASTER_PLAN.md` remains the approved primary plan. This document does not replace it or authorize implementation changes by itself.  
**Purpose:** Study the structure, progression, world variety, level pacing, and environmental mechanics of Plants vs. Zombies 2 (PvZ2), then translate useful high-level lessons into an original Farm Vs Monsters campaign.  
**Art/IP rule:** Borrow structural lessons only. Do not copy PvZ2 names, characters, artwork, animations, audio, level layouts, UI, exact mechanics presentation, or proprietary assets. Farm Vs Monsters keeps its own farmer, wife/princess, dragons, monsters, plants, story, and visual identity.

---

## 1. Research baseline: how the PvZ2 Adventure map is organized

The international PvZ2 Adventure campaign has a short home/tutorial area followed by eleven themed worlds. The currently documented world order is:

1. Ancient Egypt
2. Pirate Seas
3. Wild West
4. Frostbite Caves
5. Lost City
6. Far Future
7. Dark Ages
8. Neon Mixtape Tour
9. Jurassic Marsh
10. Big Wave Beach
11. Modern Day

The order was changed in later updates to better reflect increasing difficulty; it is not the original release order. The world list and order are documented in the Adventure Mode reference.

### 1.1 World and level count reference

| Map area | Levels in the current reference | What it contributes |
|---|---:|---|
| Player's House / opening tutorial | 5 | Teaches the core loop before the themed campaign |
| Ancient Egypt | 25 | First full themed world |
| Pirate Seas | 25 | Changes approach routes and usable ground |
| Wild West | 25 | Introduces movable plant positioning on rails |
| Frostbite Caves | 30 | Environmental hazards and lane redirection |
| Lost City | 32 | Reward-producing special tiles and projectile obstruction |
| Far Future | 25 | Linked power tiles and technology-themed enemies |
| Dark Ages | 20 | Night rules, reduced natural resource flow, graves |
| Neon Mixtape Tour | 32 | Music-driven environmental modifiers |
| Jurassic Marsh | 32 | Neutral creatures influence enemy movement |
| Big Wave Beach | 32 | Waterline and changing tide reshape safe placement |
| Modern Day | 34 | Combines earlier world mechanics through portals |
| **Total** | **317** | 5 opening levels + 312 world levels |

**Counting note:** the 11 world counts add to 312; including the five Player's House tutorial levels gives 317. Some historical references report different totals because expansion levels were later moved into Travel Log / quest content or changed by updates. Treat the table as a snapshot of the referenced international Adventure structure, not an eternal count for every game version. The Chinese edition is a different content branch and is not included here.

### 1.2 What the map does beyond listing levels

PvZ2's campaign map is effective because it communicates several layers at once:

- **Geographic progression:** each world is a distinct destination with its own visual identity.
- **Visible route:** levels form a readable path; completed, current, and locked nodes communicate progress.
- **Controlled unlocks:** the player is not shown every mission as immediately playable.
- **World identity:** each destination introduces a signature environmental rule and a recognizable enemy family.
- **Reward rhythm:** plants and other rewards arrive during progression, not only at the end of the entire campaign.
- **Difficulty layering:** a world teaches its gimmick, then combines it with enemy types and later challenges.
- **Milestones:** special encounters and a world-ending boss provide a strong finish before the next destination.
- **Replay and side content:** mini-games, endless modes, quests, and special events sit beside the main route instead of replacing it.

Official EA material describes the game as an eleven-world journey with more than 300 levels, endless zones, mini-games, and a boss at the end of each world. The detailed world order and level counts are documented by the community-maintained game reference.

## 2. World-by-world map analysis

This section records the identity and gameplay lesson of each international PvZ2 world. The final column is the transferable design lesson, not a proposal to reproduce the same content.

| # | PvZ2 world | Levels | Main identity / gameplay hook | Design lesson for Farm Vs Monsters |
|---:|---|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Ancient Egypt | 25 | Desert ruins, tombstones that obstruct direct shots, and sandstorm surprise attacks | Introduce one clear obstacle/hazard and teach how to counter it before combining it with stronger enemies |
| 2 | Pirate Seas | 25 | Ship-to-lawn planks, water that cannot be planted on, and enemies that enter from unusual positions | Change the threat entry pattern and safe planting space so the player must rethink lane coverage |
| 3 | Wild West | 25 | Minecart rails let plants be repositioned along fixed tracks | Add one controlled repositioning mechanic only after the player understands stationary defense |
| 4 | Frostbite Caves | 30 | Chilling winds can freeze plants; ice tiles can redirect enemies between lanes; surprise snowstorms | Environmental hazards should be forecast, visually readable, and counterable—not random unavoidable punishment |
| 5 | Lost City | 32 | Gold tiles reward planting; excavation enemies can obstruct or disrupt straight projectiles and plant placement | Make terrain tiles create meaningful resource/position choices, and design enemies that challenge specific attack patterns |
| 6 | Far Future | 25 | Power tiles link or spread a plant-power effect; advanced mechanical enemies | Introduce synergies that reward thoughtful placement without making one setup mandatory |
| 7 | Dark Ages | 20 | Night setting, no ordinary falling sun, mushrooms and graves that can summon enemies | A world can change the resource economy and atmosphere, but must teach the new economy early |
| 8 | Neon Mixtape Tour | 32 | Music genres/jams alter enemy behavior and the rhythm of encounters | Use a strong audiovisual identity whose environmental effects are also strategic and readable |
| 9 | Jurassic Marsh | 32 | Dinosaurs can influence, push, or otherwise alter zombie movement | Neutral wildlife or moving environmental actors can make the battlefield feel alive and change lane pressure |
| 10 | Big Wave Beach | 32 | Water and a changing tide line affect plantable space; specialist enemies disable or displace plants | A changing battlefield can create strong variety, but difficulty must be carefully controlled and signposted |
| 11 | Modern Day | 34 | Portals bring back enemies from previous worlds and combine earlier mechanics; final campaign culmination | The final world should test mastery by combining previously taught rules rather than introducing many unrelated rules at once |

**Important detail:** the order above is the current international Adventure order, not the chronological order in which worlds were released. PvZ2's older map used branching Brain Buster paths and gates; a major early redesign changed that structure. The modern map should therefore be studied as an evolving progression system, not treated as the only map structure the game ever had.

## 3. The progression pattern to learn from

A strong world is not just a background skin. PvZ2 repeatedly uses a progression pattern:

1. **Arrival / orientation:** establish the new place and its visual language.
2. **Safe introduction:** show the world rule in a low-pressure encounter.
3. **First counter:** provide or teach a response to the rule.
4. **Enemy introduction:** add a new enemy whose behavior is connected to the world.
5. **Combination:** combine the new rule with previously learned enemies.
6. **Special challenge:** change the objective or restrict the available strategy.
7. **Escalation:** increase the number and combination of threats.
8. **Elite encounter:** test the player's understanding before the finale.
9. **Boss warning:** preview the boss identity and its signature threat.
10. **World boss:** resolve the destination's main conflict and reward the player.

PvZ2 uses a variety of level types during this progression, including ordinary defense, conveyor-belt-style supply, protect/avoid objectives, restricted loadouts, and other special challenges. Its separate modes and Travel Log add replayable content outside the main world route.

### 3.1 What should be adopted

- Teach a new rule in isolation before testing it under pressure.
- Give every region a distinct visual theme, enemy family, environmental rule, and boss identity.
- Put a visible reward or unlock milestone at regular intervals.
- Make the map itself communicate where the player is, what is complete, what is locked, and what comes next.
- Use special objectives to break up repeated standard battles.
- Bring earlier enemy types back in later combinations so the player's learning remains useful.
- End each region with a boss encounter that uses that region's identity.
- Make optional challenges and endless/replay modes separate from the critical campaign route.

### 3.2 What should not be copied blindly

- Do not target 317 levels merely because PvZ2 has that many. That would be a very large production commitment.
- Do not reproduce its exact world names, map art, level layouts, enemy concepts, plants, or boss designs.
- Do not make every level longer or harder just to increase the count.
- Do not introduce several new mechanics in one encounter.
- Do not use unpredictable hazards that can destroy a run without warning or a counter.
- Do not gate the main story behind currencies or account creation.
- Do not make every world feel like the same battle with a different background.

## 4. Farm Vs Monsters — proposed alternative campaign map

This is a **secondary proposal** to compare with the primary rescue-stage plan. It preserves the approved story: a dragon and its army occupy each territory, the farmer fights through the army, defeats the dragon, rescues his wife, and gets a meaningful reunion before moving to the next territory.

### 4.1 Proposed campaign scale

For a first complete campaign map, use:

- **11 original territories** as a long-term structure.
- **10 main missions per territory** as a planning target, not an already implemented count.
- **110 main missions total** when all territories are built.
- **One territory dragon boss per territory**, separate from ordinary monster totals.
- **One wife-rescue story ending per territory**, followed by the approved reward / treasure-cave ceremony and a calm transition.
- Optional challenge missions, treasure routes, and endless survival can be added after the main route is stable.

This is deliberately smaller than the 317-level PvZ2 reference. It creates a substantial campaign while leaving room for original art, animation, level design, balancing, and boss implementation. The 10-mission pattern is a proposed Farm Vs Monsters structure, not a claim about PvZ2's exact per-world structure.

### 4.2 Proposed original territories

| Farm Vs Monsters territory (working title) | PvZ2 structural reference | Original environmental rule proposal | Enemy / boss direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Dragonfall Farm Valley | Tutorial + Ancient Egypt's introductory teaching | Gentle wind gusts and a few broken-fence obstacles; teach planting, energy, lanes, and the first dragon warning | Existing first-stage hyena family; first small dragon boss |
| 2. The Broken Coast | Pirate Seas | Shoreline/bridge entry points and occasional amphibious routes; make threat entry locations matter | Raiders, divers, and a coast dragon that attacks from offshore |
| 3. Rustrail Frontier | Wild West | A limited moving supply cart or movable farm device; do not make rooted plants walk | Fast raiders, armored wagon beasts, and a rail-yard dragon |
| 4. The Frozen Highlands | Frostbite Caves | Cold gusts temporarily slow or freeze selected cells; clear visual warning and a warm-up counter | Ice-resistant monsters and a frost dragon; fire/warmth counters |
| 5. The Overgrown Sunken City | Lost City | Ancient fertile tiles periodically produce a small resource bonus; digger enemies can temporarily block a lane | Excavator monsters, relic guardians, and a buried-city dragon |
| 6. The Clockwork Wastes | Far Future | Connected energy pylons amplify a nearby plant effect for a short, clearly bounded period | Mechanical monsters, shield drones, and a machine dragon |
| 7. The Moonlit Necropolis | Dark Ages | Night reduces passive resource generation; marked graves warn before spawning enemies | Grave callers, armored knights, and a shadow dragon |
| 8. The Festival of Echoes | Neon Mixtape Tour | A visible rhythm meter changes enemy speed/defense in predictable musical phases | Sound-driven monsters and a rhythm dragon; audio cues must have visual equivalents |
| 9. The Primeval Wilds | Jurassic Marsh | Neutral giant creatures cross selected lanes and can push enemies or disrupt formations | Ancient beasts, pack hunters, and a primal dragon |
| 10. The Flooded Green Coast | Big Wave Beach | A visible tide schedule changes which cells are safe or usable; never remove a planted unit without warning | Amphibious raiders, plant-displacing enemies, and a sea dragon |
| 11. The Riftbound Citadel | Modern Day | Portals bring back known enemy families and selected earlier hazards in controlled combinations | Elite mixed factions and the final dragon ruler |

These are original working concepts. Names, art, exact mechanics, bosses, and unlock rewards remain subject to approval and production feasibility. Do not implement a territory hazard until its UI warning, counterplay, animation, sound, and test cases are defined.

### 4.3 Ten-mission template for each territory

| Mission | Purpose | Typical structure |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Arrival and teach | A forgiving battle that introduces the territory's visual identity and one rule |
| 2 | Reinforce | Same rule with a second familiar enemy; grant a useful reward |
| 3 | Introduce faction | First new enemy archetype; show its behavior clearly |
| 4 | Teach counterplay | Give the player a tool, plant, or tactical choice that counters the new enemy |
| 5 | Special objective | A shorter mission with a changed objective, such as protect a farm object or survive a timed assault |
| 6 | Combine | Mix the territory rule with two previously learned enemy roles |
| 7 | Tactical test | Restrict or shape the loadout/available resources in a fair, explained way |
| 8 | Escalation | Introduce an elite enemy or a more complex hazard combination |
| 9 | Boss preparation | Preview the dragon, teach its signature attack, and let the player prepare |
| 10 | Dragon battle and rescue | Defeat the dragon and required followers, rescue the wife, celebrate, award rewards, then transition calmly |

The template is a planning guide. Some territories may need fewer or more missions after playtesting; the player must never be forced through filler levels just to satisfy the count.

## 5. World map UX proposal

The alternative map should borrow the clarity of a world-route map, while keeping Farm Vs Monsters' own art and story:

### Map overview
- A large illustrated world map with 11 distinct territories connected by a winding road, river, caravan route, or magical path.
- The current territory is highlighted; completed territories remain visible; future territories are dimmed but recognizable.
- Each territory has its own dragon crest, color identity, environment preview, and completion marker.
- The wife-rescue status is shown as a story milestone for each completed territory.
- Selecting a territory opens its mission path, enemy preview, region hazard explanation, available rewards, and boss silhouette/art when approved.

### Mission path
- Ten mission nodes per territory in the proposed first full version.
- Clear states: locked, available, in progress, completed, and replayable.
- Completed nodes show a medal/result; optional mastery goals can award extra cosmetics or resources, never mandatory power.
- The next mission unlocks only after the required prior mission is completed.
- A defeated dragon and rescued wife mark the territory as complete.
- The final node is visually larger and clearly identified as the dragon encounter.

### Mission preview
Before starting, show:
- Territory and mission number.
- Main objective.
- Known enemy types and any unknown-threat warning.
- Environmental rule and its counterplay.
- Suggested plant roles, not a mandatory loadout.
- Potential reward.
- Whether this is a standard, special-objective, elite, or boss mission.

### Progress persistence
- Save unlocked territories and mission completion independently of local visual state.
- Completing/replaying a mission must not duplicate one-time unlocks, coins, or rescue milestones.
- A reload must restore the exact current node and last completed mission.
- The campaign must remain playable offline/single-player without requiring account registration.

## 6. Comparison with the approved primary plan

| Topic | Primary plan | Secondary PvZ2-informed proposal |
|---|---|---|
| Core story | Every stage is a complete dragon defeat and wife-rescue adventure | Preserved, but organized into 11 themed territories |
| Current first battle | Stage 1 is the first complete battle, currently structured around five waves and a separate dragon boss | Becomes the first mission/battle of Territory 1; do not rewrite it yet |
| Campaign unit | A stage is a rescue adventure | A territory contains a route of multiple missions and ends with a dragon rescue |
| Progression | Stage N ordinary enemy target is 29 + N; dragon is counted separately | Keep the same rule as a starting balance guideline, then tune mission-by-mission; do not multiply the count blindly across ten missions |
| World variety | New territory, monster faction, dragon, and signature boss attack per stage | Adds a specific teachable environmental rule to each territory |
| Rewards | Stage rewards and treasure-cave ceremony | Adds intermediate mission rewards and optional mastery rewards while preserving exact-once accounting |
| Map | A campaign map and unlock sequence are required | Detailed world-route + 10-node territory path proposal |
| Content scale | Expand stages in sequence | Long-term 11 territories × 10 missions = 110 proposed main missions |
| Special levels | Optional challenges after core campaign | Explicitly planned mission types 5, 7, 8, and 9, plus later optional challenge routes |
| Authority | Approved master plan | Research-backed alternative for comparison only |

### Recommendation

Do not replace the primary plan now. The strongest option is to **combine the primary story and battle rules with the secondary map's world structure and level pacing**:

- Keep the farmer, wife, dragons, hyena/monster factions, treasure-cave reward ritual, and existing battle rules.
- Keep the first battle and its approved 30 ordinary monsters + separate dragon boss.
- Adopt the idea of multiple missions inside each future territory, rather than making every new territory only one isolated battle.
- Give each territory one signature environment rule, but introduce it gradually and make it counterable.
- Use the 11-territory map as a long-term campaign target, not an immediate implementation scope.
- Build and verify one territory at a time. Do not begin all 110 missions before the first complete territory is fun and stable.

## 7. Production and approval gates

Before any territory mechanic is implemented, its design record must include:

1. Player-facing explanation and visual warning.
2. Exact rule and when it activates/deactivates.
3. Which plants, farmer actions, and monster types are affected.
4. Counterplay available at that point in progression.
5. Edge cases: pause, restart, death, lane changes, overlapping effects, and save/reload.
6. Art/audio dependencies and whether the user must provide assets.
7. Data-driven tuning values rather than scattered hard-coded constants.
8. Unit tests for the pure rules and a real browser playthrough for visual behavior.

A territory is not considered complete until:
- Its mission route and unlock logic work.
- Its signature mechanic is understandable and fair.
- Its ordinary enemy roster is complete and balanced.
- Its dragon boss has distinct attacks and readable phases.
- The dragon and required followers must be defeated before rescue.
- The wife rescue, reunion, rewards, and treasure-cave sequence complete exactly once.
- Progress survives reload and replay does not duplicate rewards.
